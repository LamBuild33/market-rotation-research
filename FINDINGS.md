## 2026-10-16 — Notebook 06: market moods (Part A, build years 1999-01-04 to 2020-12-31)

Tests whether a simple "mood" label for the stock market (is SPY above or below its 200-day average, and is it more or less volatile than usual) is stable from month to month and says anything about the following month. Measures labels, not a trading strategy; no trading costs applied.

### Part A predictions (written before running anything)
- **D1.** The mood at a checkpoint matches the mood at the next checkpoint (21 trading days later) at least 60% of the time.
- **D2.** The defensive basket (equal weight XLP, XLU, XLV) has a positive average next-month excess vs the sector average in the two stormy moods combined, and a negative one in the calm uptrend.
- **D3.** SPY's average next-month return is lower at checkpoints in a downtrend (either calm or stormy) than at checkpoints in an uptrend.

Each is confirmed if the stated condition is met and contradicted if not (D2 needs both parts).

Rules and choices fixed before running: (a) trend = SPY adjusted close at or above its 200-trading-day simple average on the checkpoint = uptrend, otherwise downtrend; storminess = SPY's 21-day volatility (standard deviation of the last 21 daily returns) above the median of that same 21-day volatility over the previous 252 trading days = stormy, otherwise calm; mood = trend x storminess (four moods). (b) Checkpoints = the same 21-day grid as notebooks 02-05, starting at the first one with enough history (200 days for the trend; 21 + 252 for storminess). (c) Outcomes run from the NEXT day's close to 21 trading days after that (same one-day delay as 03 and 04); sector average = simple mean of the sector funds' changes over that span; defensive excess = mean of the XLP, XLU, XLV changes minus the sector average. (d) D1 is judged on all pairs of consecutive labeled checkpoints (no outcome needed); the chance level = sum of squared mood shares, reported as context. (e) D2 and D3 average over checkpoints (one observation per checkpoint), with the two stormy moods pooled for D2 and the two uptrend moods and the two downtrend moods pooled for D3; D3 only needs the downtrend average to be lower. (f) Rough 95% ranges resample checkpoint dates (2,000 draws, fixed seed). Checkpoints without a full next-month outcome inside the build years are dropped from the outcome results and counted. No method changes after seeing results.

## 2026-10-16 — Notebook 05: who moves first (Part A, build years 1999-01-04 to 2020-12-31)

Tests whether one market's return today helps predict another market's return next period (lead-lag): "A leads B" = correlation of A's return in period t with B's return in period t+1. Daily (23 symbols, BTC-USD excluded) and weekly (24 symbols).

### Part A predictions (written before running anything)
- **M1.** Daily: at most 10% of ordered pairs (A leads B, A != B) have a full-period lag-1 correlation outside the 95% noise band.
- **M2.** Daily: no ordered pair has a full-period lag-1 correlation larger than 0.10 in absolute size.
- **M3.** Weekly: at most 10% of ordered pairs have a full-period lag-1 correlation outside the 95% noise band.

Each is confirmed if the stated condition is met and contradicted if not.

### Part A results (run 2026-10-16, notebook `05_who_moves_first.ipynb`)
- **Data range and setup:** build years only, 1999-01-04 to 2020-12-31. Daily: 5,535 days, 23 symbols (BTC-USD excluded: its 00:00 UTC close is about 4 hours after US stocks close, which would fake a lead); 506 ordered pairs. Weekly: 1,147 weeks (last trading day of each Monday-Sunday week), 24 symbols; 552 ordered pairs. "A leads B" = correlation of A's return in period t with B's in t+1; noise band +/-1.96/sqrt(n) for each pair; a pair-year belongs to the year of the follower's period (needs 120+ days daily, 26+ weeks weekly). I recomputed five daily pairs independently with plain pandas (SPY->EEM, XLK->XLP, XLC->UUP, XLE->XLU, GLD->UUP) and SPY's autocorrelation; all matched the notebook to six decimals.
- **M1, daily: at most 10% of ordered pairs outside the noise band: CONTRADICTED.** **415 of 506 pairs (82.0%)** are outside the band (chance alone would give about 5%).
- **M2, daily: no ordered pair larger than 0.10 in size: CONTRADICTED.** The largest is **0.291 (XLC leads UUP, -0.291, n=638)**, and **108 pairs** exceed 0.10. The top of the list is dominated by XLC and XLRE, which have only 638 and 1,316 days (XLC's window includes the 2020 crash). Among long-history pairs the largest are SPY leads EEM -0.167 (n=4,461), XLV, XLF, ITA, XLI, XLK, KRE and XLB leading EEM (-0.13 to -0.16) and SPY leads KRE -0.125.
- **M3, weekly: at most 10% of ordered pairs outside the noise band: CONTRADICTED.** **155 of 552 pairs (28.1%)** are outside the band; the largest is XLRE leads XLU -0.282 (n=272); 67 pairs exceed 0.10 in size.
- **Why so many pairs are outside the band: it is the market, not leaders.** Daily returns of the stock funds are negatively autocorrelated (a market-wide reversal): SPY leads SPY is **-0.092**, 18 of 23 symbols' own autocorrelations are outside the band (all negative; e.g. XLF -0.105, KRE -0.101, EEM -0.119, XLC -0.249). Because nearly every fund moves with the market, the market's reversal shows up in almost every ordered pair (the heatmap is red almost everywhere; only TLT as a leader is blue). It is concentrated in crisis years: SPY's autocorrelation was -0.15 (2007), -0.12 (2008) and -0.32 (2020), and the share of pairs outside the band was 30.0% (2007), 30.7% (2008) and **81.6% (2020)** against 3% to 21% in other years; the average lag-1 correlation in 2020 was -0.18. Weekly: 12 of 24 symbols' autocorrelations are outside the band (mostly negative, e.g. XLRE -0.227, XLP -0.133, XLF -0.128; USO +0.095 is positive). So the contradicted predictions mostly reflect one market-wide pattern, which the market-removed view (below) strips out.
- **Steady leads (plain view; outside the band over the full period AND the same sign in at least 75% of the years the pair exists):** **daily 115** (78 without XLC and XLRE, which exist only 3 and 5 build years; 70 of those 78 are negative, 8 positive) and **weekly 64** (46 without XLC, XLRE and BTC-USD; 53 negative, 11 positive). Strongest long-history daily ones, with years and share of years with the same sign: SPY->EEM -0.167 (18 years, 78%), XLV->EEM -0.156 (89%), XLF->EEM -0.148 (89%), ITA->EEM -0.146 (15 years, 100%), XLI->EEM -0.145 (83%), XLK->EEM -0.140 (83%), KRE->EEM -0.134 (93%), SPY->KRE -0.125 (15 years, 80%), KRE->XLK -0.120 (87%), XLK->XLP -0.112 (22 years, 86%), QQQ->XLP -0.110 (86%); the strongest positive ones all have TLT as leader: TLT->XLP +0.094 (18 years, 83%), TLT->XLU +0.088 (83%), TLT->EEM +0.074 (83%). Weekly examples: XLF->KRE -0.150 (15 years, 93%), XLU->KRE -0.138 (87%). Full lists with yearly numbers: `results/05_steady_leads_daily_plain.csv`, `05_steady_leads_weekly_plain.csv`. **For scale** (`same_day_corr` column), the same-day correlations of these pairs are 0.5 to 0.9, so the next-day effects (-0.1 to -0.17) are small and opposite in sign: yesterday's move partly reverses today. They are the Part B candidates, but they are mostly the same market-wide reversal, not specific "A leads B" relationships.
- **Does the market-removed view change the picture? Yes, a lot (information only).** Daily: **118 of 506 pairs (23.3%) outside the band** (down from 82.0%), largest 0.114 (SPY->IWM -0.114; SPY->XLV +0.107); only 2 pairs above 0.10; 23 steady leads, the strongest SPY->SMH -0.094 (21 years, 86%), SPY->QQQ -0.092 (22 years, 77%), TLT->XLU +0.072 (18 years, 78%), XLF->XLI +0.062 (22 years, 86%), XLB->SMH +0.057 (21 years, 90%). Weekly: **59 of 552 (10.7%) outside the band** (down from 28.1%), 11 steady leads (XLP->SPY -0.132 over 22 years, 77%; GLD->BTC-USD -0.125 over 6 years; XLK->SMH -0.110; KRE->SPY +0.109; XLV->KRE +0.106). So after removing the market, weekly is close to noise (10.7% vs the 5% chance level) and daily still shows a few small effects (still above chance at 23%), none above 0.114. In this view SPY's leftover is "S&P 500 vs the equal-weight sector average" (a big-company tilt), so the SPY-led results are partly about that tilt.
- **Early years and thin trading:** the check is mixed. The share of pairs outside the band was 20.9% in 1999, 15.4% in 2000 and 11.5% in 2001 but 4.5% in 2002, and follower averages (average lag-1 correlation over all leaders) were positive for several sector funds in 1999-2003 and negative afterwards (XLV +0.058 vs -0.041, XLI +0.042 vs -0.029, XLY +0.031 vs -0.029, XLB +0.034 vs -0.020; single years XLV 2000 +0.187, XLB 2001 +0.132, XLI 2001 +0.113). That fits a "price catches up a day late" pattern in 1999-2001, but it is modest and much smaller than the 2007-2008 and 2020 effects. EEM shows the same early-vs-later flip (+0.091 early, -0.070 later) and likely also reflects timing: EEM trades during US hours while its Asian holdings have already closed.
- **Cost reminder:** a lag correlation of 0.1 explains about 1% of next-day variance, and a few hundredths is unlikely to survive the 0.10%-per-trade cost. No trading rule was tested here.
- **Self-pairs:** full tables in `results/05_self_pairs_autocorrelation.csv` (daily and weekly, plain and market-removed, full period and by year).
- **Caveats:** the noise band assumes independent periods, which is only approximately true for returns; with hundreds of pairs, some outside the band are expected by chance; XLC (131 weeks, 638 days) and XLRE (272 weeks, 1,316 days) have short histories and wide bands; the market-removed fit uses the equal-weight sector average, which includes the sector funds themselves. XLK, XLY, XLC, XLF and XLRE changed contents in 2016 and 2018 (XLRE left XLF in Sept 2016; XLC took Alphabet and Meta from XLK and Netflix and Disney from XLY in Sept 2018), so pairs spanning those dates compare slightly different baskets. SMH before 2011-12-20 is the old Semiconductor HOLDRS.
- **Files:** `results/05_daily_lag1_plain.csv`, `05_weekly_lag1_plain.csv`, `05_top15_daily_plain.csv`, `05_top15_weekly_plain.csv`, `05_daily_lag1_by_year_plain.csv`, `05_weekly_lag1_by_year_plain.csv`, `05_steady_leads_daily_plain.csv`, `05_steady_leads_weekly_plain.csv`, `05_self_pairs_autocorrelation.csv`, `05_by_year_summary.csv`, `05_follower_profile_daily.csv`, `05_summary_shares.csv`, the `*_market_removed.csv` equivalents, and `05_heatmap_daily_lag1.png`.
- **Takeaway:** On 1999-2020 data there is no sign that one market reliably moves before another once you look beyond the market itself. The three predictions failed because most pairs (82% daily, 28% weekly) show small negative lag-1 correlations, which come from a market-wide next-day reversal in the stock funds, strongest in 2007-2008 and 2020. Once the market is removed the weekly result is near noise (10.7%) and the daily one is small (largest 0.114). The steady leads are candidates for Part B, with the caveat that most are that same reversal.
